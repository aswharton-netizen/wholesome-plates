Food Safety & Compliance

HACCP FOOD SAFETY PLAN

Effective Date: August 2026

Purpose

Wholesome Plates maintains a Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) based food-safety system designed to identify and control reasonably foreseeable biological, chemical, and physical food-safety hazards throughout our operation.

The plan covers food under WPC's control from:

Approved Supplier → Receiving → Secured Storage → Preparation → Cooking → Holding/Cooling → Packaging → Load-Out → Transportation → Client Delivery

Wholesome Plates predominantly utilizes same-day production and delivery. Certain menu items may occasionally be safely cooled, frozen or refrigerated, reheated, and served at a later time. Appropriate cooling and reheating controls apply whenever these processes are used.

Management Responsibility

Management is responsible for maintaining this plan, training employees, reviewing records, verifying corrective actions, and reviewing the food-safety system periodically.

Employees are responsible for following procedures applicable to their positions, conducting assigned monitoring, accurately completing required records, and immediately reporting deviations.

STOP → HOLD → NOTIFY → CORRECT → DOCUMENT

Potentially unsafe food will not knowingly be distributed.

1. Purchasing

Food is purchased from approved commercial suppliers.

Purchasing and supplier documentation is maintained as appropriate to support food safety and traceability.

2. Receiving — Control Point

Deliveries are received by authorized WPC personnel, including ServSafe-certified kitchen personnel.

Applicable refrigerated and frozen products are checked for appropriate temperature and condition.

Products are also evaluated for packaging integrity, contamination, damage, tampering, and other unacceptable conditions.

For approved key-drop deliveries, applicable products are inspected and temperatures verified when WPC personnel arrive at the facility.

Corrective Action

Products that do not meet applicable food-safety standards are rejected or isolated pending management determination.

Receiving documentation is maintained as appropriate.

3. Secured Refrigerated, Frozen & Dry Storage

WPC inventory is stored in designated company storage.

Refrigerated and frozen inventory is maintained within locked WPC cages located inside shared walk-in coolers/freezers.

Cold TCS foods are maintained at 41°F or below. Frozen foods are maintained frozen.

Shared walk-in temperature-monitoring equipment provides facility-level temperature information, while WPC personnel remain responsible for evaluating food under WPC's control.

Raw foods are separated appropriately from ready-to-eat products to reduce cross-contamination risk.

Products are appropriately covered, labeled, dated, and rotated.

Corrective Action

When storage conditions fall outside acceptable parameters, WPC will evaluate affected inventory based upon actual product temperature, duration, food type, and applicable food-safety requirements.

Food that cannot be established as safe will not be used.

4. Preparation & Cross-Contamination Control

Before production begins, WPC personnel clean and sanitize applicable shared food-contact surfaces and equipment.

Employees follow procedures addressing:

  • handwashing;
  • appropriate glove and utensil use;
  • employee illness reporting;
  • raw versus ready-to-eat food separation;
  • clean and sanitized utensils and equipment;
  • appropriate food handling;
  • chemical storage and use; and
  • allergen awareness.

Production areas and applicable equipment are cleaned and sanitized after use.

5. Cooking — Critical Control Point

WPC uses calibrated probe thermometers to verify internal cooking temperatures.

Infrared thermometers may be used for appropriate surface or equipment checks but are not used as the sole method for determining required internal food temperatures.

Applicable minimum internal temperatures include:

  • Poultry: 165°F
  • Ground meats: 155°F
  • Seafood and whole cuts of meat: 145°F
  • Other foods: temperatures required by applicable food-safety requirements.

Temperatures are checked after cooking and documented according to WPC procedures.

Corrective Action

Food that has not achieved its required minimum temperature will continue cooking until the requirement is achieved.

Food that cannot be safely brought into compliance will not be served.

6. Cooling — Critical Control Point

Although WPC primarily uses same-day production, some foods are occasionally prepared for later service, including items that may be safely cooled and refrigerated or frozen.

When cooling cooked TCS foods, WPC uses approved rapid-cooling procedures. Food will be cooled from 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, and 70°F to 41°F or below within an additional 4 hours.

Appropriate methods may include shallow pans, dividing food into smaller portions, use of commercial refrigeration/freezing equipment, ice baths, or other approved rapid-cooling methods appropriate to the product.

Cooling time and temperature will be monitored when this process is used.

Corrective Action

Food failing to meet required cooling parameters will be appropriately reheated and recooled when permitted and safe to do so, or discarded.

7. Reheating — Critical Control Point

When previously cooked and cooled TCS food is reheated for hot holding, WPC will rapidly reheat the product to the applicable required temperature, generally 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours.

Internal temperature is verified using a calibrated probe thermometer.

Food that cannot safely meet the reheating requirement will not be served.

8. Hot & Cold Holding

Hot TCS foods are maintained at 135°F or above. Cold TCS foods are maintained at 41°F or below.

Applicable temperatures are monitored during production and holding.

9. Load-Out — Critical Monitoring Point

WPC verifies applicable food temperatures during load-out before food leaves the commercial kitchen.

Prepared foods are wrapped/sealed and transferred from appropriate cooking, refrigeration, or warming equipment into insulated catering carriers suitable for hot or cold transportation.

Load-out temperature information is documented as part of WPC's delivery records.

Food outside applicable safety limits will be corrected or replaced before dispatch.

10. Transportation

Prepared food is transported in insulated catering carriers designed to maintain appropriate hot or cold conditions and protect food from contamination.

Food remains wrapped/sealed during transportation as appropriate.

Drivers are trained in appropriate food-handling, transportation, delivery, security, and temperature procedures applicable to their responsibilities.

11. Delivery — Critical Monitoring Point

Applicable food temperatures are verified upon delivery to the client location.

WPC maintains delivery receipts containing temperature information, providing documentation of the condition of food at delivery and supporting WPC's traceability system.

If food arrives outside applicable safety parameters, personnel will notify management and follow WPC corrective-action procedures.

Unsafe food will not knowingly be served.

12. Thermometer Use & Calibration

WPC utilizes probe thermometers to measure internal food temperatures and infrared thermometers for appropriate surface/equipment monitoring.

Food thermometers used for critical temperature measurements are maintained in proper working condition.

Applicable thermometers are checked/calibrated daily, including use of an ice-water calibration method as appropriate.

A thermometer that cannot be properly calibrated or produces unreliable measurements will be removed from critical food-temperature monitoring until corrected or replaced.

13. Cleaning & Sanitation

WPC cleans and sanitizes applicable shared food-contact surfaces and equipment before and after production.

Cleaning and sanitation procedures address:

  • food-contact surfaces;
  • utensils;
  • production equipment;
  • prep surfaces;
  • prevention of chemical contamination;
  • appropriate sanitizer use; and
  • verification of sanitizer concentration when applicable.

The shared commercial kitchen operator also maintains facility-level cleaning and sanitation responsibilities. WPC maintains responsibility for sanitation of its production areas, equipment, and food-contact surfaces during its operations.

14. Allergen Awareness & Special Dietary Requirements

WPC identifies applicable major allergens associated with menu items and communicates allergen information through its menu and program procedures.

When notified of an applicable participant allergy or dietary restriction, WPC may remove the identified allergen from applicable food production or menus when operationally appropriate.

Because WPC operates within a shared commercial kitchen in which other authorized businesses may handle allergens, the facility is not represented as allergen-free and WPC cannot guarantee the complete absence of trace allergen cross-contact.

For contracts requiring medically necessary meal modifications or substitutions, WPC will work with the client to address documented requirements in accordance with applicable USDA Child Nutrition Program requirements and the governing contract.

15. Recall & Traceability

WPC's Food Recall & Traceability Policy is incorporated into this HACCP program.

WPC maintains:

  • traceability from receipt through delivery;
  • product hold and isolation procedures;
  • designated primary and backup recall contacts;
  • 24/7 emergency recall accessibility;
  • client notification procedures;
  • public website recall-notification capability;
  • documented product disposition; and
  • annual mock recall procedures.

16. Biosecurity

WPC's Biosecurity & Food Supply Protection Policy is incorporated into this HACCP program.

Particular attention is given to the shared-kitchen environment through secured WPC inventory, locked storage, controlled access, pre-production inspection and sanitation, tamper awareness, and chain-of-custody controls.

17. Corrective Actions

When a food-safety requirement or critical limit is not met, employees will:

  1. STOP the affected process when appropriate.
  2. HOLD potentially affected food and prevent distribution.
  3. NOTIFY designated management.
  4. CORRECT the condition when a safe corrective action is available.
  5. DOCUMENT applicable deviations and corrective actions.

When food safety cannot reasonably be established, the food will be discarded rather than served.

18. Verification & Records

Management periodically reviews implementation of this plan through applicable records including:

  • receiving and receiving-temperature records;
  • refrigerator/freezer monitoring;
  • cooking-temperature records;
  • cooling records when applicable;
  • reheating records when applicable;
  • load-out temperatures;
  • delivery temperatures and receipts;
  • thermometer calibration records;
  • sanitation records;
  • corrective-action documentation;
  • employee training records;
  • recall/hold records; and
  • annual mock-recall documentation.

19. Employee Training

WPC employees receive food-safety training appropriate to their responsibilities. Training includes, as applicable:

  • personal hygiene and illness reporting;
  • receiving and storage;
  • temperature monitoring;
  • probe thermometer use;
  • thermometer calibration;
  • cross-contamination prevention;
  • cooking;
  • cooling and reheating;
  • hot/cold holding;
  • sanitation;
  • allergens and special dietary requirements;
  • packaging and transportation;
  • delivery;
  • corrective actions;
  • recall and traceability; and
  • biosecurity.

Training is documented. Management-level food-safety oversight is provided by ServSafe Manager-certified personnel, with additional WPC production personnel maintaining applicable food-safety training/certification.

20. Shared Facility Documentation

WPC maintains or obtains applicable documentation concerning the licensed shared commercial kitchen facility as appropriate, which may include:

  • current health/operating permits;
  • pest-control documentation;
  • facility sanitation procedures;
  • equipment/facility maintenance information;
  • emergency procedures; and
  • shared-kitchen operating requirements.

Facility-level responsibilities do not replace WPC's responsibility for food under WPC's custody.

21. Plan Review

This HACCP Food Safety Plan will be reviewed periodically and when significant changes occur to WPC's menu, production methods, equipment, facility, transportation system, applicable regulations, or food-safety processes.

The plan will also be reviewed following significant food-safety incidents, recalls, or findings indicating that a control requires modification.
